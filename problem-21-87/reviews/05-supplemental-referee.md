## Exact theorem and sharpness

The candidate proves the full universal statement: for every nonnegative
integer (d), if a finite group (G) has a nonempty family (mathcal H) of
subgroups such that (d(H)leq d) for all (Hinmathcal H) and

\[
  \gcd\{\lvert G:H\rvert:H\in\mathcal H\}=1,
\]

then (d(G)leq d+1).  It does not restrict (G) to a soluble or primitive
class, alter (d) in a quotient, or weaken the conclusion to (d+2).

The newly supplied sharpness witness is correct.  In (S_3), subgroups of
orders (2) and (3) are cyclic and have indices (3) and (2), while
(S_3) is noncyclic and two-generated.  Thus no universal (d)-generator
conclusion can replace (d+1).

## Independent proof reconstruction

### 1. Arithmetic and quotient kernel

- Since every subgroup index divides (lvert Gvert), the gcd of an arbitrary
  family is attained by a finite subfamily.
- The gcd is one exactly when, for every prime (p\midlvert G\rvert), some
  member (H) has (p\nmidlvert G:H\rvert); equivalently, (H) contains a
  Sylow (p)-subgroup of (G).
- If (N\unlhd G), then (HN/N) remains at most (d)-generated and
  (lvert G/N:HN/N\rvert=lvert G:HN\rvert) divides
  (lvert G:H\rvert).  Therefore any common divisor of the image indices is a
  common divisor of the original indices.  The direction of divisibility is
  the one needed for quotient inheritance.
- A Sylow subgroup intersects a normal subgroup in a Sylow subgroup and maps
  to a Sylow subgroup under a surjection.  The order calculation in Lemma 2.3
  is correct.

### 2. Minimal counterexample and crown reduction

Fix (d\geq1), put (m=d+1\geq2), and choose a least-order counterexample.
Every quotient by a nontrivial normal subgroup is (m)-generated.  Lucchini's
augmentation-ideal result gives (d_G(I_G)\leq m); hence
(d(G)=d_G(I_G)+\operatorname{pr}(G)>m) forces positive presentation rank.
Gruenberg's soluble-normal-subgroup theorem then excludes every nontrivial
soluble normal subgroup.

Dalla Volta--Lucchini's critical-group theorem applies with the exact
quantifier (d(G/N)\leq m) and, importantly for (d=1), with the endpoint
(m=2).  It gives (G\cong L_k), where (k=f_L(m)\geq2).  The abelian-socle
branch would put the nontrivial soluble normal subgroup (A^k) in (G), so
(A) is nonabelian.  Since (L) is a proper quotient, (d(L)\leq m).

For nonabelian (A), the published Eulerian-function identity is

\[
  f_L(m)=1+\frac{\phi_L(m)}
  {\lvert\Gamma_{L,A}\rvert\phi_{L/A}(m)}.
\]

The manuscript defines

\[
  P_{L,A}(m)=\frac{\phi_L(m)}
  {|A|^m\phi_{L/A}(m)},
\]

so the displayed crown formula follows by direct substitution, with no
missing floor, ceiling, or power of (lvert A\rvert).  The
Detomi--Lucchini bound (P_{L,A}(m)\geq1/2) therefore yields

\[
  k\geq1+\frac{|A|^m}{2\lvert\Gamma_{L,A}\rvert}. \tag{L}
\]

### 3. Pointwise Sylow-centralizer estimate

Let (S) be nonabelian simple and (P\in\operatorname{Syl}_2(S)).  For an
involution (t) with (S=\langle P,t\rangle), put
(D=C_{\operatorname{Aut}(S)}(P)), where the centralizer is pointwise.
Sylow conjugacy puts an involution in (P) in the (S)-class of (t), so
that class is (D)-invariant.  The stabilizer (D_t) is trivial because it
fixes the generating set (P\cup\{t\}) pointwise.  Hence

\[
  |D|\leq|t^S|=\lvert S:C_S(t)\rvert\leq|S|/2.
\]

For a nonabelian monolithic socle
(A=S_1\times\cdots\times S_n), restriction embeds
(\Gamma_{L,A}) in (operatorname{Aut}(A)).  The injectivity proof is valid:
an automorphism trivial on both (A) and (L/A) differs from the identity on
each (x\in L) by an element of (C_A(A)=Z(A)=1).  An automorphism fixing a
Sylow (2)-subgroup of (A) pointwise cannot permute the simple direct
factors, because every factor has a nontrivial Sylow (2)-subgroup.  The
factorwise estimate therefore gives

\[
  \lvert C_{\Gamma_{L,A}}(R)\rvert\leq|A|/2
  \qquad(R\in\operatorname{Syl}_2(L)). \tag{C}
\]

No setwise centralizer is substituted for the pointwise centralizer at any
stage.

### 4. Coordinate-orbit packing

Sylow coverage supplies an at-most-(d)-generated (H\leq L_k) containing a
Sylow (2)-subgroup (T).  After padding with identities, its (d) generators
give (k) coordinate tuples in a single fiber of (L^d\to(L/A)^d), whose
cardinality is (|A|^d).

Two coordinate tuples cannot be in one (Gamma_{L,A})-orbit.  Such a relation
would extend, using one fixed automorphism, from the displayed generators to
every word in them.  But (T\cap A^k) is a direct product of nontrivial Sylow
(2)-subgroups in the (k) crown coordinates and contains an element
supported in only one of the two coordinates, contradicting the relation.

The image (T_j) of (T) in the (j)-th copy of (L) is Sylow because the
coordinate projection is surjective.  A tuple stabilizer fixes the projected
image of (H), and hence (T_j), pointwise.  By (C), every one of the (k)
distinct orbits has at least
(2\lvert\Gamma_{L,A}\rvert/|A|) elements.  Packing them in the fiber gives

\[
  k\leq\frac{|A|^{d+1}}{2\lvert\Gamma_{L,A}\rvert}
    =\frac{|A|^m}{2\lvert\Gamma_{L,A}\rvert}, \tag{U}
\]

which contradicts (L).  The separate (d=0) argument is also correct.
