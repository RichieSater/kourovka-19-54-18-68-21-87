## Exact result checked

The candidate proves the statement printed as Kourovka Problem 21.87: if a
finite group \(G\) has a family of subgroups generated by at most \(d\)
elements and their indices have greatest common divisor one, then
\(d(G)\leq d+1\).  The manuscript states the hypothesis with
\(\lvert G:H\rvert\), treats a nonempty family, and also handles the harmless
endpoint \(d=0\).  It does not weaken the conclusion to \(d+2\), fix \(d\), or
restrict \(G\) to a soluble, primitive, or almost simple class.

## Independent proof-path reconstruction

1. The index condition is equivalent to coverage of a Sylow \(p\)-subgroup by
   an at-most-\(d\)-generated member for each \(p\mid |G|\), and it passes to
   every quotient with the same \(d\).
2. In a least counterexample, put \(m=d+1\).  Every proper quotient is
   \(m\)-generated.  Lucchini's augmentation-ideal theorem gives
   \(d_G(I_G)\leq m\), hence the counterexample has positive presentation
   rank.  Gruenberg's result then excludes every nontrivial soluble normal
   subgroup.
3. Dalla Volta--Lucchini's theorem makes the counterexample the critical
   crown-based power \(L_k\).  Its socle is nonabelian, \(L\) is a proper
   quotient, and therefore \(m\geq d(L)\).  The exact crown formula and the
   Detomi--Lucchini probability bound give

   \[
     k\geq 1+\frac{|A|^m}{2|\Gamma_{L,A}|}.
   \]
4. Guralnick's theorem yields an involution \(t\) with
   \(S=\langle P,t\rangle\) for a Sylow \(2\)-subgroup \(P\) of every
   nonabelian simple socle factor.  Its involution class gives
   \(\lvert C_{\operatorname{Aut}(S)}(P)\rvert\leq |S|/2\), and restriction
   to the simple factors gives the corresponding
   \(\lvert C_{\Gamma_{L,A}}(R)\rvert\leq |A|/2\) bound.
5. An at-most-\(d\)-generated subgroup containing a Sylow \(2\)-subgroup of
   \(L_k\) yields \(k\) distinct \(\Gamma_{L,A}\)-orbits in a fiber of size
   \(|A|^d\).  Each orbit has size at least
   \(2|\Gamma_{L,A}|/|A|\), so

   \[
     k\leq\frac{|A|^{d+1}}{2|\Gamma_{L,A}|}
       =\frac{|A|^m}{2|\Gamma_{L,A}|},
   \]
   contradicting the strict lower bound.

Every transition in this reconstruction appears in the manuscript.  The two
uses of Sylow transport, the supported-coordinate element, and the inclusion
of the projected Sylow subgroup in the tuple stabilizer's fixed subgroup are
all explicit.

## Acceptance-critical source audit

- **Lucchini 1992:** Theorem 2 supplies exactly the augmentation-ideal bound
  for a family whose indices have no common divisor; the
  presentation-rank identity is recorded there as well.
- **Gruenberg/Lucchini 1990:** item 2.1 states that quotienting by a soluble
  normal subgroup preserves \(d(G)\) when the presentation rank is positive.
- **Dalla Volta--Lucchini 1998:** Theorem 1.4 covers \(m\geq2\) and the exact
  nontrivial-normal-subgroup quantifier.  Theorem 2.7 covers
  \(m\geq d(L)\) and has the nonabelian Eulerian-function formula used after
  normalization.  The endpoint bars on both printed inequality signs were
  checked visually because the PDF text layer drops them.
- **Detomi--Lucchini 2013:** the official repository abstract states
  \(P_{L,A}(m)\geq1/2\) for \(m\geq d(L)\); the public author slides state the
  stronger \(53/90\) estimate in the nonabelian-socle branch actually used.
- **Guralnick 1986:** Burness--Guralnick's public introduction explicitly
  records the original Sylow \(2\)-subgroup plus involution theorem.  Sylow
  conjugacy transports the existential pair to the chosen Sylow subgroup.

The source hypotheses align with the manuscript, and the abelian/nonabelian
socle alternatives are exhaustive.  No low-parameter or simple-group family
is left for an implicit finite check.
